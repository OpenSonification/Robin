Explain the map

Robin is an 11 by 11 grid from minus 5 to 5 on both axes. Moving up raises the pitch, and moving left or right moves the sound between your headphones. With VoiceOver off, touch a cell once or drag over the map to hear position tones, then double-tap a cell to plot. With VoiceOver on, touch or swipe to hear cells; Robin waits for VoiceOver to say "button" before playing the sound. Then use VoiceOver's double-tap to plot. If iOS has not started web audio yet, the first cell activation starts it. An empty cell plays its position tone on its own; plotted shapes add their sounds on top.

Touch controls

These controls are for touchscreen phones and tablets. There are no keyboard or directional-arrow controls in this version.

  1. Choose the shape you want to plot.
  2. With VoiceOver off, touch a cell once to hear it. The first touch also starts Robin audio.
  3. Keep one finger down and drag across the map to hear each cell you cross. Double-tap a cell to plot the selected shape.
  4. With VoiceOver on, touch or swipe to hear cells, then use VoiceOver's double-tap on the focused cell to plot. Robin briefly delays cell sounds so they follow the "button" announcement. If the browser has not yet allowed sound, the first cell activation starts it.

Focused cell actions

These browser-accessible buttons provide the delete and playback actions for the cell most recently focused or selected.

How to use it

Arrow keys
Move around the grid from anywhere on the page.
S C T D
Select square, circle, triangle, or diamond.
Shift
Plot the selected shape in the focused cell.
Shift + arrows
Plot the selected shape while moving.
Backspace
Remove the most recently plotted point in the cell.
1
Play the current row from left to right.
2
Play the current column from bottom to top.
3
Play only plotted shapes, one column at a time.
4
Play only plotted shapes, one row at a time.
Space
Turn the screen black or restore the graphics.
Tab
Move between the controls on the left.
Enter / Space
Use the button that currently has focus.

Headphones make the left-to-right movement easiest to hear.
